# Arcades Everywhere

## Description

While it might seems that games played in public disappeared with the Arcade, public forms of gameplay still exist within contemporary culture. Perhaps an oversimplification, but what started with the arcade has diverge into at least two notable paths, one niche and one diffuse. 

To begin with the diffuse. Perhaps the arcade and play in public never really left, it just become synonymous with smart phones; an act of public computing many of us constantly experience. If you own an iPhone you might even note that Apple's game subscription service is called Apple Arcade. People are playing games everywhere: in parks, on trains, in class, or waiting in line. While these mobile games might not look visually similar to the games of early arcades (See: Love in Deep Space), many of these games include similar tactics of getting players into a flow state to continually extract value in the form of payment. While not all mobile games, many of these games focus on a small definable loop that can be played endlessly for high scores (See Candy Crush Saga, Subway Surfers, Fruit Ninga, Clash of Clans, Flappy Bird). At the same time, artists like Zach Blas, are exploring the limits of the casual game space as attempts to see if interested work can be made when we focus on quick play that happens in public.

As for the niche, games in public can now be seen widely within art galleries. From the works of Danielle Braithwaite Shirley to Lawrence Lek, artists are exploring the aesthetics of games and interactive media to think through a variety of cultural, political and economic ideas. Within galleries, these games are played in public with viewers watching and gamers performing. This can paritcularly be seen in the work of Daniel ....asks you xyz. Aside from art galleries, Independent game makers are still continuing to produce arcades and arcade cabinets. Oftentimes less focused on conceptual and aesthetic questions and more focused on game play, these arcade games are continuing to push the bounds of public play. A variety of these arcade games can be found at indepent arcade venues such as Wonderville in NYC. One notable project is the arcade cabinet Nave by Videogamo. This is a traveling arcade cabinet that maintains highsorces across locations. Notably academic explorations of public game play include [UCLA's Game Art Festival](https://festival.games.ucla.edu/) and NYU's [No Quarter](https://gamecenter.nyu.edu/events/no-quarter/) Exhibitions.

For this project, your aim is to great a work of interactive media art that is designed to be played in public. This project may take many forms, from a work that may embarrass players/onlookers, a work that will inspire cheer and applause, to a work one plays constantly, forever, whether waiting in line or laying in bed. Considering, the games discussed above and during this unit, your project should be simple to understand, easy to begin playing,, and if you choose, difficult to advance after a certain point. Of course, these parameters can be tweaked and altered depending on the publics you have in mind. Your final submission will include and work of interactive media that will be played and critiqued and class, as well as a piece of writing that situates your work within the class readings.
